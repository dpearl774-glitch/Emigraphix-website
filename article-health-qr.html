How Health Professionals Can Use QR Codes More Effectively

A QR code is most useful when it removes a step: book, chat, find, read or register.

A QR code is useful when it saves somebody a step. If scanning it takes a patient, client or visitor exactly where they need to go, it can make a flyer, poster or business card much easier to use.

For health professionals, that can mean more than simply linking to a website.

1. Let people start a WhatsApp conversation

A WhatsApp QR code can open a chat with your number already selected and, if you want, a short message already typed. That works well on consultation flyers, appointment cards and event materials.

Keep the prefilled message simple — for example, “Hello, I would like to enquire about a nutrition consultation.”

2. Link directly to booking

If you use an online booking page, the QR code can take the person straight there instead of asking them to search for your page manually.

Label the code clearly: “Scan to book an appointment” is more useful than displaying an unexplained square in the corner.

3. Share patient or campaign resources

A poster cannot hold every detail. A QR code can lead to a trusted page, downloadable guide, registration form or campaign resource where people can learn more.

For health information, check the destination carefully before printing the code. The page should be current, credible and suitable for the audience.

4. Make location easier to find

A QR code can open a map link for a clinic, pharmacy, laboratory or event venue. That can be especially useful on printed appointment cards or outreach materials.

5. Use QR codes as a supporting route, not a mystery

Tell people what will happen when they scan. “Scan for directions,” “Scan to register” or “Scan to WhatsApp us” gives the code a purpose.

And where the information is important, do not hide everything behind the QR code. Keep essential contact details or instructions visible as well.

Static QR codes do not normally expire

A static QR code stores its destination directly in the code. The image can keep working indefinitely, but the destination still needs to remain valid. If the phone number, website or file link changes, the old code will continue pointing to the old destination.

Useful rule: before printing hundreds of copies, scan the final QR code from more than one phone and test the entire journey — not just whether the square scans.

The best QR code is not the fanciest one. It is the one that makes the next action easier.
