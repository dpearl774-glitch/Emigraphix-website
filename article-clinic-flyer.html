What Should Be on a Clinic or Health Service Flyer?

A useful flyer should help someone understand your service quickly — and know what to do next.

A clinic flyer can look beautiful and still leave someone wondering what the clinic actually does. The strongest flyers do the opposite: they make the important information easy to pick out in a few seconds.

Before worrying about colours, icons or photographs, start with the question a potential patient is likely to have: What is this service, is it for me, and what should I do next?

Start with the one thing the flyer is meant to do

A flyer should not try to carry every piece of information about your practice. Decide what the flyer is for. Are you introducing a new clinic? Promoting a screening service? Announcing consultation days? Encouraging people to book?

Once that is clear, the headline becomes easier to write and the rest of the information can support it.

Include the information people actually need

For most clinic or health-service flyers, the essentials are straightforward:

  • Your clinic or professional name.
  • The service being offered. Say it plainly rather than hiding it inside a slogan.
  • Who the service is for, when that matters.
  • Where and when the service is available.
  • How to contact or book. A phone number, WhatsApp link, booking link or QR code should be easy to find.

If there is an important condition — for example, appointment only — it should not be buried at the bottom in tiny text.

Do not make every sentence compete for attention

People scan before they read. Give the headline, service name and call to action more visual weight. Supporting details can be quieter. When everything is bold, large or brightly coloured, nothing feels important.

WHO's communication guidance encourages communicators to identify the most important message, use familiar language and make the desired action clear. That is a useful test for a flyer too: can someone tell what matters without reading it three times?

Use health visuals because they help, not because there is empty space

A photograph or illustration should support the message. A nutrition service might benefit from a relevant food or consultation image; a maternal-health message may need a clear, respectful illustration. Decorative medical symbols everywhere can make a flyer busy without making it clearer.

A quick test: show the flyer to someone for five seconds, take it away, then ask what service was being offered and what they were supposed to do. If they cannot answer, the hierarchy probably needs work.

Give the flyer one clear next step

“Call to book,” “Send us a WhatsApp message,” “Visit the clinic,” or “Scan to register” is stronger than leaving several disconnected contact details with no direction. The goal is to reduce the amount of thinking the reader has to do.

A clinic flyer should feel professional, but professionalism is not the same as squeezing in more information. Often, the clearer flyer is the one that knows what to leave out.
